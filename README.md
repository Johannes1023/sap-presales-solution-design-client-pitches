# 🎯 SAP Pre-Sales, Solution Design & Client Pitches

## 📌 Project Overview

This project sample summarizes several enterprise SAP pre-sales engagements I worked on during my time at PwC and NTT DATA.

The opportunities focused on complex business challenges in warehouse management, logistics and sales processes. Typical project volumes ranged from approximately **€0.5 million to more than €1 million**.

I worked as Technical Solution Consultant and Pre-Sales SME within multidisciplinary bid teams. My role was to understand the client's business problem, translate it into a viable SAP solution, support mock-ups and pilot scenarios and present the proposed approach to technical experts and senior decision-makers.

I contributed significantly to **three successful engagements at PwC and four at NTT DATA**.

---

## 🎯 Business Challenge

Large enterprise clients often entered the selection process with a clear operational problem, but without a fully defined technical solution.

Typical challenges included fragmented warehouse processes, limited inventory transparency, inefficient material flows or complex sales and order management processes.

At the same time, the opportunities were highly competitive. We regularly faced established consulting and technology providers, including firms such as **Accenture, Capgemini and Tata Consultancy Services (TCS)**.

The challenge was therefore not only to propose a technically strong SAP solution, but to convince the client that our team had the clearest understanding of the problem, the most credible implementation approach and the lowest execution risk.

Key topics included:

- Understanding complex requirements within short discovery cycles
- Translating business challenges into SAP solution concepts
- Developing compelling mock-ups and pilot scenarios
- Differentiating the proposal from competing providers
- Presenting the solution to senior management and technical experts
- Explaining implementation approach, risks and dependencies transparently
- Balancing solution quality, delivery effort and commercial competitiveness

# 🔍 Customer Discovery

The first priority was to understand the client's actual business problem before discussing specific SAP functionality.

Discovery typically followed a short and intensive cycle:

```text
Business Challenge
        │
        ▼
Discovery Workshops
        │
        ▼
Current Process & Pain Points
        │
        ▼
Business Requirements
        │
        ▼
Technical Requirements
        │
        ▼
Solution Options
```

Typical questions included:

- Where does the current process create delays or unnecessary manual work?
- Which process steps cause the highest operational effort?
- Which systems, interfaces and stakeholders are involved?
- Which requirements are genuinely business-critical?
- Where can standard SAP processes replace custom complexity?
- What would define a successful implementation for the client?

The goal was to demonstrate that we understood the client's operation before proposing a target solution.

---

# 🏗️ Solution Design

After discovery, I worked with the consulting team to translate the requirements into a concrete solution concept.

For SAP EWM opportunities, the focus included:

- Goods receipt and putaway
- Inventory transparency
- Picking and warehouse execution
- Material movements
- Warehouse process optimization
- Integration with purchasing and logistics processes

For SAP SD opportunities, the focus included:

- Order-to-cash processes
- Sales order management
- Pricing and conditions
- Delivery processing
- Billing
- Integration with logistics and finance

A key design principle was to balance customer-specific requirements with standardized SAP functionality and avoid unnecessary customization.

---

# 🧪 Mock-Ups & Proof of Concept

Written concepts alone were often not sufficient in competitive pre-sales situations.

Where appropriate, we created mock-ups, prototypes or pilot scenarios to make the proposed solution tangible.

```text
Business Requirement
        │
        ▼
Solution Concept
        │
        ▼
Mock-Up / Pilot
        │
        ▼
Client Demonstration
        │
        ▼
Feedback
        │
        ▼
Solution Refinement
```

I worked with SAP specialists and developers to define the scenarios that should be demonstrated and made sure that the pilot focused on the client's most important pain points.

The objective was not to build the full solution during pre-sales, but to provide enough evidence that the proposed approach was technically feasible and operationally relevant.

# 🗺️ Delivery Approach & Implementation Plan

One of the strongest factors in successful bids was the clarity of the proposed implementation approach.

Clients were not only evaluating what we wanted to build. They also wanted to understand **how we would deliver it**.

We therefore made the delivery plan highly transparent:

```text
Discovery
   │
   ▼
Solution Design
   │
   ▼
Build & Configuration
   │
   ▼
Integration
   │
   ▼
Testing
   │
   ▼
Deployment
   │
   ▼
Stabilization
```

The proposal clearly showed:

- Project phases
- Responsibilities
- Major milestones
- Dependencies
- Delivery assumptions
- Key risks
- Decision points
- Expected client involvement

This gave decision-makers a clearer view of what would happen after contract signature.

A strong solution concept could still lose if the implementation approach looked vague. Making the delivery path understandable reduced perceived execution risk and helped build confidence in the team.

# 💬 Technical Client Pitch

The final presentation was often one of the most demanding parts of the sales process.

The audience could include senior executives, business process owners, IT leadership and highly experienced SAP specialists.

My role was to explain the proposed solution clearly while being able to move into technical detail when challenged.

Typical discussions included:

- Why a particular process design was chosen
- Which SAP capabilities would be used
- Where configuration or custom development might be required
- How the solution would integrate with the existing environment
- Which implementation risks needed to be managed
- How the project would be structured and governed
- Why our approach was preferable to competing proposals

The goal was not simply to present slides, but to give the client confidence that the team understood both the solution and the realities of implementation.

---

# ⚔️ Competitive Differentiation

The bids were frequently contested by major consulting firms and system integrators.

Winning therefore required differentiation across several dimensions:

- Quality of the proposed solution
- Understanding of the client's business
- Relevant SAP expertise
- Credibility of the delivery team
- Commercial attractiveness
- Quality of the implementation plan
- Transparency around risks and dependencies

Price mattered, but it was rarely sufficient on its own.

One of the strongest differentiators was presenting a delivery approach that was concrete enough for the client to understand how the project would actually run.

This reduced uncertainty and made the proposal feel executable rather than theoretical.

# 💰 Commercial & Delivery Perspective

The technical solution also needed to be realistic from a commercial and delivery perspective.

I supported the wider pre-sales team with topics such as:

- Project scope
- Work packages
- Implementation approach
- Required expertise
- Effort estimation
- Delivery assumptions
- Major implementation risks

This helped connect the promises made during the sales process with something the delivery organization could realistically execute afterwards.

Across PwC and NTT DATA, the opportunities I worked on typically represented **mid-six-figure to low-seven-figure project volumes**.
